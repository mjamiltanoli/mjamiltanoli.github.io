BOX 7 - SOLUTION - TWO INTERNETS - FULL EXPLANATION

7. Solution: Two Internets + Neutral Satellite Under UN

Two Internets, Two Different Governance Systems, One Humanitarian Purpose.

The Problem: When war occurs, when an earthquake strikes, or when a flood hits, communication networks may fail during emergencies. When communication fails, doctors cannot reach patients, rescue teams cannot find routes, and families cannot contact each other. Lives are not lost only because help did not exist. Lives are lost because communication did not exist.

The Solution: One internet is not enough. The Jamil Doctrine therefore proposes two internets. These two internets will not replace each other. They will operate under separate governance systems. Internet 2 is proposed as a logically isolated network with dedicated capacity, not necessarily a fully separate physical network.

  • INTERNET 1 - NATIONAL SOVEREIGN INTERNET: The internet of each sovereign state. Each state shall govern its own internet in accordance with its own constitution and laws. The national government shall remain responsible for its domestic infrastructure, ISPs, cybersecurity, and legal framework. No external authority shall replace lawful national control. This internet includes the State, Military, Banks, Hospitals, Power Grid, Citizens, Business, and Education.
  • INTERNET 2 - UN-NEUTRAL HUMANITARIAN INTERNET: This second internet shall not be under the control of any single state. It shall not be owned or controlled by any single private corporation. It shall operate under neutral United Nations governance. Its sole purpose shall be the protection of human life and saving lives. It shall not become a commercial or political internet.
  • NEUTRAL SATELLITE INFRASTRUCTURE - SUPPORT FOR INTERNET 2: The second internet is proposed to be supported by satellite infrastructure because many terrestrial networks depend on cables, towers, and ground-based equipment. Cables can break, towers can fall, and terrestrial networks can be disrupted or shut down. Satellite infrastructure can provide an independent alternative to terrestrial networks, reducing dependence on vulnerable cables and towers. However, resilience against jamming, cyberattacks and other disruptions must be addressed through redundant systems and international cooperation. Its functions shall include: Connecting areas without terrestrial internet, Maintaining communication during disasters, Supporting emergency medical operations, Providing communication during humanitarian crises, and Connecting rescue organizations.

How It Will Work on a Normal Day: On a normal day, Internet 1 shall operate, and each state shall operate its own internet.

How It Will Work in an Emergency: In an emergency, Internet 2, through satellite, shall operate only for hospitals, rescue teams, and people in need, to help maintain essential communication.

GUARANTOR: HEAD OF STATE - NOT CORPORATION - Proposed Governance Principle

Foundational Statement: One Internet belongs to every country's sovereign governance under its own laws. The second Internet belongs to humanity's humanitarian purpose, under neutral United Nations governance, controlled by neither a single country nor a single corporation, and dedicated exclusively to saving human lives.
